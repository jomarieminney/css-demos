09 · Scroll-driven · scroll() + view() + animation-range

Scrolling Floofs

Animations that run on scroll position instead of a clock. The bar along the top is how far down you are, the cards arrive as they come into view, and a row of polaroids goes sideways while you scroll down. Scroll back up and it all plays in reverse: it's position, not time.

Arriving on cue

Each card has its own view() timeline: 0% when its top edge peeks over the bottom of the window, 100% when it has gone off the top. animation-range uses just the first slice of that, so a card has finished arriving by the time a third of it is showing. The photo inside drifts at its own pace, and the rating fills as it comes up the screen.

Sideways, by scrolling down

This section is a couple of screens tall. While it passes, the box inside sticks to the window and the row is moved sideways by exactly how much it overflows. The timeline is the section's, shared with the row by name.

This browser doesn't have scroll timelines, or you've asked for less motion, so here it's a row you scroll sideways instead.

Counting up as you scroll

The number is a custom property, --tally, animated from 0 to 72 on a view() timeline, so it counts up as it comes into view. Custom properties can't normally be animated, because the browser doesn't know what they hold; @property registers this one as an <integer>, so it steps through whole numbers. And because content can't show a custom property directly, counter() prints it.

Progressive enhancement, live

What this browser is giving you right now. Every tick is decided by @supports, not JavaScript. The @property chip is the exception: @supports can't reliably ask about @property yet, so that chip checks that a registered property actually works instead. Where scroll timelines are missing, or you've asked your system for less motion, the page is all there and simply holds still: the progress bar and the back-to-top button step aside, and the strip is a row you scroll sideways.

CSS · graded live by @supports

  • animation-timeline: scroll() the progress bar, the back-to-top button
  • animation-timeline: view() the cards, the photos, the ratings
  • animation-range which slice of the trip each one uses
  • view-timeline a named timeline the strip shares with its row
  • position: sticky the strip holds still while it scrolls
  • 100cqi how far the row has to travel
  • @property gives --tally a type, so it can count
  • prefers-reduced-motion motion is opt-in

Full disclosure: scroll timelines are the newest thing on this page, so check the ticks above before you promise them to anyone. Where they're missing, you get the still version. The count-up also needs @property, to give --tally a type (an <integer>) so it can be animated at all.
